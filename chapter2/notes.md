# Chapter 2: Periodic Classification of Elements — one-page revision

## Basics you need first
- **Atomic number (Z)** = number of protons = number of electrons (neutral atom).
- **Shells** K, L, M, N hold at most 2n² electrons: 2, 8, 18, 32.
- **Electronic configuration**: Na (11) = 2, 8, 1 · Cl (17) = 2, 8, 7 · Ar (18) = 2, 8, 8.
- **Valence electrons** = electrons in the outermost shell. They decide valency and properties.
- Metals lose electrons (electropositive); non-metals gain electrons (electronegative); metalloids are in between (B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te, Po).

## The story of classification
| Year | Scientist | Idea | Problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1817 | Dobereiner | **Triads**: middle element's atomic mass ≈ average of the other two (Li 7, Na 23, K 39) | Only a few triads found |
| 1866 | Newlands | **Law of octaves**: every 8th element is like the 1st (sa re ga ma pa dha ni sa); H to Th, 56 elements | Worked only up to Ca; Co and Ni in one box; unlike elements together; no room for new elements |
| 1869 | Mendeleev | **Properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic masses.** 63 elements; used atomic mass + formulas of oxides and hydrides | See demerits below |
| 1913 | Moseley | **Properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic numbers.** (Modern periodic law) | — |

**Mendeleev's merits:** left gaps and predicted eka-boron (Sc), eka-aluminium (Ga), eka-silicon (Ge); corrected atomic masses (Be 14.09 → 9.4); noble gases fitted as a zero group.

**Mendeleev's demerits:** position of hydrogen not fixed; no place for isotopes; Co (58.9) before Ni (58.7); atomic mass doesn't increase regularly.

## Modern periodic table
- 7 periods (rows), 18 groups (columns).
- Blocks: s (groups 1–2), p (13–18), d (3–12, transition), f (lanthanides and actinides, inner transition).
- Zig-zag line: metals on the left, non-metals on the right, metalloids along the line.
- **Period number = number of shells.** **Same group = same valence electrons.**
- Group = valence electrons (groups 1, 2) or valence electrons + 10 (groups 13–18).
- Elements per period: 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32.

## Trends (understand the two reasons)
| Property | Across a period → | Down a group ↓ |
|---|---|---|
| Shells | Same | Increase |
| Valence electrons | 1 → 8 | Same |
| Valency | 1 → 4 → 0 | Same |
| Atomic radius | Decreases | Increases |
| Metallic character | Decreases | Increases |
| Non-metallic character | Increases | Decreases |

- **Across:** nuclear charge increases, same shell → electrons pulled closer.
- **Down:** a new shell is added each time → atom gets bigger, outer electrons are easier to lose.
- Radius in picometres (1 pm = 10⁻¹² m). Li 152, Be 111, B 88, C 77, N 74, O 66 · Li 152, Na 186, K 231, Rb 244, Cs 262.

## Memory tricks
- History: **3, 8, mass, number** (triads, octaves, Mendeleev, Moseley).
- Group 2 (Be Mg Ca Sr Ba Ra): *Beta Mange Car, Scooter Baap Raazi*.
- Halogens (F Cl Br I): *Fat Cats Bring Ice*.
